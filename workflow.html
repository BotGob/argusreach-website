Build posture One lane first. Expand after trust is earned. The first build should prove the workflow can run cleanly, visibly, and repeatedly before it grows into a larger system.
Step 1

Show us the work that keeps getting rebuilt manually.

Start with a recurring report, spreadsheet process, dashboard update, research task, competitor data pull, policy monitor, document review, CRM cleanup, finance/admin task, or operational status update.

Reports and dashboards

Management reports, client-facing materials, operational summaries, executive dashboards, and recurring performance updates.

Data and research collection

Websites, shared folders, portals, PDFs, workbooks, CRMs, email, government sources, or other systems.

Manual coordination

Approvals, exceptions, handoffs, follow-ups, review queues, and status updates that slow teams down.

Reliability Layer

Reliable workflows need more than a prompt.

The useful system is the combination of logic, review points, memory, logs, and a clear support path. That is what makes the workflow repeatable.

What stays attached to the workflow

Each build should keep source expectations, business rules, reviewer notes, exceptions, and approval logic close to the work so the team is not re-explaining the process every time.

Rules and calculations Reviewer notes and rulings Logs, approvals, and escalation points

Step 3

Build around the highest-value part.

Sometimes the right answer is an agent. Sometimes it is AI setup, a dashboard, report generator, internal portal, automation, SharePoint/Excel workflow, or a combination of several pieces.

Custom agent

Collect inputs, prepare work, check logic, draft summaries, track changes, and route exceptions.

Dashboard or reporting engine

Turn recurring data into a clean executive view, client-facing material, or repeatable report.

AI-assisted workflow

Give the team a controlled way to review, approve, update, and monitor the process without replacing judgment.

Step 4

Deploy with controls. Improve from there.

The first version should make the workflow easier to run, reduce manual coordination, and prove the system can be trusted. Once that works, we expand carefully.

01

Review and validation

The system is checked against real examples before it is trusted with recurring work.

02

Human oversight

Approvals, logs, and clear handoff points stay in place where judgment matters.

03

Maintenance path

We define who provides new data, where it lives, how often it refreshes, and what happens if something breaks.
