FAQ

Direct answers for operators and executives.

What ArgusReach builds, where human review stays in place, what a good first project looks like, and why custom systems outperform generic AI tools for serious workflow work.

Best way to start

Bring one workflow that is slow, repetitive, inconsistent, or not getting done at all. That is the right starting point for a serious build.

  • Recurring report or research process
  • Client follow-up or sales support workflow
  • Internal operations or approval routing process
  • Monitoring task that should happen reliably but does not

What does ArgusReach actually build?

Custom AI agent systems built around real business workflows. That can include research and monitoring agents, reporting systems, sales support workflows, client follow-up systems, internal routing, bookkeeping support, and cross-system automation.

How is this different from a generic AI tool?

Generic tools give you a broad interface. We build a system around your actual process, your data, your outputs, and your review points. The result is better fit, better reliability, and more useful work.

Do you replace the systems we already use?

No. In most cases we build above the tools and data already in place. The goal is to create an intelligence layer that connects existing systems and produces better output.

Where do humans stay involved?

Where judgment, approval, or risk matter. Human-in-the-loop is part of the design. We do not treat oversight as an afterthought.

What kinds of firms are a good fit?

Professional firms and operating teams with recurring workflows, meaningful source data, and a clear need to improve output or add capability without immediately adding headcount.

What is not a good fit?

Generic chatbot requests, full autopilot expectations, vague AI experimentation with no owner, or projects that are not intended to become a real operating system inside the business.

What happens after launch?

We stay involved. Support is part of the model because systems need monitoring, refinement, and adjustments as the workflow evolves.

Start with the workflow, not the tool.

If there is a process inside the firm that is costing time, creating inconsistency, or sitting undone because nobody owns it, that is the conversation to have first.
